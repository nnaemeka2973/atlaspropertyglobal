Why America's Secondary Luxury Markets Are Drawing a New Generation of Buyers

As housing preferences evolve, affluent buyers are examining a broader range of American cities and communities for primary residences, second homes and long-term real estate ownership.

For years, the American luxury-property conversation has centered on a familiar group of markets: Manhattan, Los Angeles, Miami and San Francisco. Those markets remain important, but the definition of a luxury real estate destination in the United States is becoming broader.

Affluent households are increasingly evaluating properties according to a combination of lifestyle, accessibility, privacy, local amenities and long-term ownership considerations. That has expanded the conversation to a wider collection of cities and regional markets.

A Broader Map of American Luxury

Markets such as Austin, Nashville, Scottsdale, Charleston, Denver, Naples and other established metropolitan and resort communities have developed distinctive luxury segments. Each market has its own economic profile, housing supply, buyer base and development patterns.

For buyers, that variety can be useful. A person looking for a waterfront residence may have very different priorities from an investor evaluating a multifamily property or an executive searching for a primary residence near employment centers.

The luxury market is no longer defined by a short list of cities. Increasingly, it is defined by the quality of the opportunity and the reasons a buyer wants to own there.

Lifestyle Is Becoming Part of the Investment Conversation

Real estate remains a highly local asset. Property values and rental performance can differ substantially between neighborhoods, even within the same city. For that reason, buyers increasingly consider the broader characteristics of a market alongside the property itself.

Access to transportation, employment, schools, recreation, healthcare, restaurants and cultural amenities can all influence how a location performs as a place to live and as a real estate market.

What International Buyers Should Know

The United States remains a major destination for international real estate purchasers. Buyers located outside the country may be interested in residential property, second homes, commercial assets or longer-term investment opportunities.

The process, however, can differ from purchasing property in a buyer's home country. Ownership structures, taxes, financing, insurance, title procedures and professional representation can all require careful review.

International buyers should work with appropriately qualified U.S. real estate, legal, tax and financial professionals before entering a transaction.

Looking Beyond the Headline Price

A property's asking price is only one part of the ownership equation. Buyers should consider the condition of the property, carrying costs, taxes, insurance, financing terms, maintenance requirements, local market conditions and the intended use of the property.

For investors, additional questions may include rental demand, operating expenses, vacancy risk, property management and applicable regulations. No market or property should be treated as a guaranteed investment.
